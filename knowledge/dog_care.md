## Daily exercise and walks
Most adult dogs need at least 30 minutes to 2 hours of activity a day, depending on breed, age, and health. High-energy breeds (herding and sporting dogs) usually need the upper end. Split exercise into two or more walks rather than one long outing. Puppies and senior dogs do better with shorter, more frequent walks. Avoid hard exercise right before or after a large meal.

## Feeding schedule
Adult dogs are usually fed twice a day, about 8 to 12 hours apart. Puppies under six months often need three or four smaller meals a day. Keep meal times consistent, and measure food instead of free-feeding to prevent weight gain. Fresh water should always be available.

## Grooming and nail trims
Brush short-coated dogs about once a week and long-coated dogs several times a week to prevent mats. Most dogs need a nail trim every 3 to 4 weeks; if you can hear nails clicking on the floor, they are too long. Bathe only when needed, roughly once a month for most dogs, since frequent baths can dry the skin.

## Dental care
Brushing a dog's teeth daily is ideal, and a few times a week still helps. Use toothpaste made for dogs; human toothpaste can contain xylitol, which is toxic to dogs.

## Foods that are dangerous for dogs
Never feed dogs chocolate, grapes or raisins, xylitol (a sweetener in sugar-free gum and some peanut butters), onions, garlic, macadamia nuts, or alcohol. If a dog eats any of these, contact a veterinarian or an animal poison control line right away.
